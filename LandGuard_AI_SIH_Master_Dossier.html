1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement Context

Over 68% of major infrastructure projects in India suffer from severe time overruns, with pre-construction land acquisition and statutory clearance disputes accounting for more than 52% of total delay durations (CAG Performance Audit Reports & MoSPI). Existing administrative platforms (such as Bhoomi Rashi and PM GatiShakti) rely on retrospective milestone tracking that flags delays only after statutory deadlines elapse.

LandGuard AI delivers a proactive, predictive decision-support system. It integrates Time-to-Event Survival Analysis (Random Survival Forest 80% + Cox Proportional Hazards 20%), PostGIS spatial geofencing, and Explainable AI (Local Feature Attribution) to provide district administrators with multi-horizon risk probabilities, statutory SLA countdowns, and real-time policy simulation capabilities.

2. Mathematical & Statistical Foundations

Standard regression and decision-tree regressors fail in pre-construction monitoring due to the Right-Censoring Problem: ongoing corridors have not yet concluded, meaning their true delay is only known to be greater than or equal to their elapsed duration (\(T_i \ge t_i\)). Standard models falsely treat elapsed time as final completion time, introducing severe survivorship bias.

1. Cox Proportional Hazards (CPH) Model:   h(t | X) = h₀(t) · exp(∑ βⱼ Xⱼ)
• Provides semi-parametric interpretability for statutory hazard multipliers (e.g. Forest Clearance lapse generates HR = 2.45x).
• Partial Likelihood estimation eliminates baseline hazard parameters to compute exact statutory coefficients.
2. Random Survival Forest (RSF) Ensemble (80% Weight) — Ishwaran et al. (2008)
• Non-parametric ensemble of 100 survival trees using Log-Rank split statistics on right-censored pairs (Tᵢ, δᵢ).
• Captures high-dimensional non-linear interactions (e.g. Schedule V Tribal Land × Active Court Cases > 5).
• Evaluated via Concordance Index: C-Index = 0.89 (89% pairwise risk ranking accuracy).
3. Multi-Horizon Cumulative Delay Probability:   S(t | X) = [S₀(t)]^(Hazard Ratio)
• 30-Day Delay Probability: P(Delay_30d) = 1 - S(30 | X)
• 90-Day Delay Probability: P(Delay_90d) = 1 - S(90 | X)   [Synchronized 1-to-1 with Composite Risk Score]

6. SIH Judges Quick Defense Q&A Script

Q1: Why didn't you just use standard Linear Regression or Random Forest Regressor?

Answer: "In infrastructure land acquisition, ongoing projects represent right-censored time-to-event data. If a project is at Month 14 and ongoing, treating 14 months as the final completion duration creates severe survivorship bias. We implemented Random Survival Forests (Ishwaran et al. 2008) using log-rank splitting on censored data, paired with Cox Proportional Hazards, achieving a Concordance Index of 0.89."

Q2: How do you guarantee your model isn't a black box?

Answer: "Every prediction generates Local Feature Attribution (SHAP) decomposing risk into exact statutory factors (e.g. litigation velocity, R&R lag, compensation gap). In addition, we compute a Tri-Model Consensus Score across RSF, CPH, and Logistic Milestone heads to confirm low model disagreement."

Q3: How does your system ensure legal compliance with Indian Law?

Answer: "Our backend strictly adheres to the RFCTLARR Act 2013: Section 4 SIA timelines, Section 11 notifications, Section 19 declarations, Section 26/27 circle rate rural multipliers (1.0x–2.0x), Section 30 100% Solatium, Section 30(3) 12% p.a. interest, and Section 41/42 Schedule V tribal protections."
